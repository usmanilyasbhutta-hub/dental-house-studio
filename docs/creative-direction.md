## 1. The direction in one line

**Premium clinical, calm, precise, trustworthy, contemporary: editorial healthcare with cinematic restraint.
The story happens on screen; words support it.**

Cinematic means staging, light, composition, action and sound, not darkness, slow motion or letterboxing by default.
Premium can be warm, lively and surprising. Restraint means deliberate choices, not static footage.
DHS is a clinic, not an education-only channel: the reward can be a useful answer, a satisfying process, a reveal,
a community moment or an honest human moment.

## 2. Before making anything

Order: **context → campaign DNA → DHS anchor → one concept → hero → typography → proof → campaign styling →
commercial layer → subtraction → checks.** Not "content → layout → polish".

**Campaign DNA** (ask only what the brief does not already answer; 4-6 at most, treat answers as meaning, not words to paste):
1. What real moment in the patient's life are we entering?
2. What should the viewer feel or understand before reading any detail?
3. What single change do we want in the viewer (notice, understand, trust, compare, save, enquire, book)?
4. What must stay unmistakably DHS here?
5. What outside world may it borrow from, and what must it never become?
6. What real proof makes the promise believable?

**Mode map** (decide what the piece *is* before styling it):
- Communication job, one primary: STOP · ORIENT · PROVE · EXPLAIN · REASSURE · DESIRE · CONVERT · CELEBRATE.
- Genre, precise (clinical proof, treatment reveal, premium cosmetic, consultation/trust, educational micro-story,
  procedural process film, offer ad, myth correction, celebration...). Never just "cinematic" or "premium".
- Emotional contract: viewer state at start → at end, the payoff, and the emotional risk (what it must not feel like).
- Energy curve beat by beat. Energy must not stay uniformly high; contrast creates emphasis.

**DHS anchor before campaign styling.** At least one real DHS behaviour carries the piece: authentic clinical
evidence, the clinic itself, crop language, typography behaviour, colour restraint, motion grammar or tone.
A logo alone is not an anchor. Campaign styling (wedding, festival, season) is a temporary skin over DHS, never a replacement.

**One concept** you can say in one sentence. If it needs several unrelated tricks to feel interesting, simplify or renew it.

## 3. Real first, illustration to frame, never generated proof

- Real DHS footage and photography are the default. Make real material presentable (crop, exposure, white balance,
  stabilising, pacing, sound, typography, masking that does not change the clinical truth). Do not replace usable
  real media because synthetic is easier.
- **Real where it proves; designed or illustrative where it frames; repeat an asset only when it adds new information.**
- Give every real asset one job: case/result = proof · logo/signage = identity · treatment detail = process ·
  clinic interior/exterior = place · clinician-patient = human trust. The first real asset is the highest-value
  evidence for *this* message, not automatically a clinic interior.
- Fewer, stronger real assets beat repeated medium ones. Before reusing one, ask what new job it does.
- Generated or constructed material: education, mechanisms, previsualisation, clearly illustrative scenes. Never
  patients, results, before/afters, clinician actions, equipment capability, testimonials or clinic events presented
  as evidence. Never match-cut illustration and patient imagery so they read as the same case. A small disclaimer does
  not rescue a misleading sequence: redesign it.
- Faceless by default: never require the owner or a doctor on camera. When a doctor supplies footage, it is real
  media and leads (see §12 on T07).

## 4. Clinical accuracy in procedure animation (owner feedback, Nov 2026)

- Instrument geometry, real-world scale, working tip, operating angle, motion, material and sequence must be
  clinically accurate. Reference genuine equipment before modelling (`blender-clinical` skill).
- Check continuity and contact with the actual teeth, gingiva and materials.
- Physically implausible props, made-up instruments, unnatural transitions, visible composites and AI-looking results
  are **defects**, even when technically generated. Generic or "fake dental 3D" is slop; accurate reconstructions are not.
- Approved elements stay unchanged unless the owner asks for a change.
- Judge exported frames and actual motion; never assert an effect works without looking.

## 5. Hierarchy, layout and sequence

- Hero first: **hero visual / hook → core message → proof → offer → details.** Do not default to
  headline → paragraph → icons → rounded card → price → CTA.
- One dominant entry point per frame; one primary job per frame. Whitespace is not unfinished space.
- Mobile-first; asymmetric/editorial compositions over centred template stacks; large image areas, decisive crops;
  never make every frame a boxed card.
- Plan a sequence as a progression of roles (e.g. identity → idea → proof → principle → place → action), not repeated
  layouts with new copy. Vary information density on purpose. A typography-led frame is a finished frame.
- Cover/opening discipline: when the opening carries proof or identity, price, discount, inclusions and terms do not
  compete with it.
- CTA discipline: the final frame makes the action unmistakable first, the contact route second, price/terms third
  (unless the brief is explicitly price-first). One action, not save + share + follow + book.

## 6. Opening and pacing (video)

- The opening must give a visible reason to watch the next moment: an action already in progress, an outcome-first
  visual, an unresolved problem, an unexpected contrast, a human moment, or a defensible bold statement.
  A headline, a logo or a random camera move is not enough on its own. **No logo intros.**
- Before full production, consider three genuinely different mechanisms (prediction/choice · concealed reveal ·
  transformation · satisfying craft · scale surprise · everyday comedy with consenting staff/props), each as
  FIRST FRAME → EXPECTED ACTION → CHANGE → PAYOFF → DHS CONNECTION. Prototype the first 2-3 seconds and reject a weak premise early.
- Hook → retain → reward: set an expectation, progress towards it, fulfil it. Keep visual progress after the hook; never
  replace an engaging scene with a stack of text cards.
- Let the action decide the runtime. A simple visual idea often lands in 8-18 s; a one-idea explainer 15-25 s as a test;
  longer only when the payoff needs it. No compulsory cut every second, countdown or voiceover.
- The piece should still make sense **muted**; sound should add timing, texture, anticipation or payoff.

## 7. Motion language

Decide it before animating, per piece: calm ↔ kinetic, soft ↔ sharp; a small family of timings; one easing family;
one transition family; stagger only when it helps; and **what stays still**.

- Motion hierarchy: **Hero** (the one movement that wins) · **Support** (clarifies sequence/meaning) · **Texture**
  (atmosphere only). Support and texture never compete with the hero.
- Begin from the real action in the footage. Prefer: hard cuts, restrained 2-6 frame flash cuts, subtle push-ins,
  macro crops, light speed ramps, masked reveals, object/hand wipes, matched camera vectors, action matches,
  sound-led cuts, occasional freeze-frame annotation.
- Avoid: transition packs, zoom-whoosh, random spins, page flips, neon streaks, glitch, bounce-everything,
  constant motion, animating every word, effects that compensate for weak story.
- Kinetic type is a meaning-and-rhythm layer, used only when it clarifies meaning, reinforces rhythm or strengthens
  hierarchy. If it reads better still, keep it still. When footage is the proof, type stays subordinate.
- Peak budget: do not let picture, type, motion and sound all peak together. Choose: picture peaks while type
  steps back; or the picture holds still while one word lands and the music drops.
- Valid expert decisions: `NO TYPOGRAPHY` · `NO ADDED MOTION` · `HARD CUT` · `NO MUSIC HERE` ·
  `KEEP NATURAL PAUSE` · `NOT REPAIRABLE: RESHOOT`.

## 8. Typography, colour and logo

- Type: one coherent family plus at most one role-based second; editorial serif only where it strengthens hierarchy
  and stays readable on a phone. Sentence case for longer headlines, explanations and subtitles; caps only for short
  labels. Avoid generic default-looking type, thin faux-luxury scripts, novelty type (see §12 on condensed caps).
- Captions: large, high contrast, short lines; no karaoke clutter unless it serves the concept (see §12).
- Colour has roles: dominant field, supporting content, restrained emphasis, within the DHS palette
  (charcoal green `#333C39`, dark olive ≈ `#3A3521`, ivory/bone neutrals, plus the bronze accents in `src/brand.ts`).
  Test text on its real background; never rely on colour alone for meaning. Grade to unify, never to change
  clinical appearance; no heavy teal-orange.
- Logo: the supplied master stays whole, undistorted, un-glowed, un-rotated, not redrawn, not boxed. Do not stamp it on
  every frame: opening identity moment, final frame, an occasional anchor, a discreet watermark only when needed
  (see §12 on the HUD bug).

## 9. Sound

Dialogue/clarity first. Real contact and texture, selected foley, music, voice and intentional silence, synced to
actual events, not to every edit. No fake patient distress or invented treatment sounds presented as real.
Music original or licensed (golden rule 6), optional, never a double soundtrack. If audio cannot be heard in review,
say audio QA is pending.

## 10. Genre notes

| Genre | Emotional arc | Avoid |
|---|---|---|
| Premium cosmetic / whitening | anticipation → polish → confidence | bargain urgency, sparkle, fake whiteness, over-retouching |
| Consultation / trust | uncertainty → being heard → clarity → trust | fast cutting, melodrama, intimidating clinical sound |
| Treatment / before-after proof | problem → process credibility → reveal → confidence | misleading wipes, exposure changes, **non-matched framing presented as a direct comparison** |
| Education | confusion → recognition → simple insight → takeaway | lecture pacing, screen-filling text |
| Celebration / community | anticipation → warmth → peak → memory | (stronger music and expressive type are allowed at real peaks) |

## 11. Review: inspect → diagnose → correct → compare

Review the **actual export** (contact sheet from the encoded file: ~0/1/2 s, midpoint, payoff, CTA), once with sound
and once muted. Then:

- **Tests:** 2-second first impression · phone size · without the logo (still DHS? still clinical/dental?) ·
  truth/source (real proof real, illustration not documentary) · asset role (each clip a new job) · restraint (what
  can go with no loss?) · CTA obvious and proportionate · thumbnails together show progression, not repetition ·
  hero motion wins · transitions motivated · enough stillness · payoff earns the setup.
- **Diagnose before editing**, one tag: Concept · Hierarchy · DHS identity · Category identity · Asset role ·
  Typography · Motion · Pacing · Sound · Proof · CTA.
- **Severity:** Blocker (truth, privacy, consent, clinical misrepresentation, wrong brand asset, unreadable/misleading)
  → Major (weak concept, wrong hierarchy, generic identity, redundant assets, dated motion, poor pacing, weak payoff)
  → Minor (spacing, crop, micro-type). Fix blockers and majors first; do not stack fixes.
- **Preserve / Modify / Renew:** keep approved strong layers; repair the weak layer only; rebuild when the concept is
  the problem. Compare old vs revised side by side and keep the one that communicates better, not the one with more effects.
- **No-slop gate:** rework when several appear together: glow/gradients, floating UI cards, generic giant sans
  headlines, decorative icons, stock smiles, fake dental 3D, template symmetry, too many hierarchy levels, every empty
  area filled, unrelated effects, luxury clichés, synthetic imagery standing in for proof.
- **Output over compliance:** a piece that follows every rule but feels generic, dated, AI-made or unclear is not finished.
- **Learning:** one good execution is a campaign lesson (log it in `docs/taste.md`), not a new rule. It graduates here
  only when it works across different briefs or the owner adopts it.
