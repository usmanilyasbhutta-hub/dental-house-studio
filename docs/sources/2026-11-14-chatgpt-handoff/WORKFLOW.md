## 2. Hormozi-inspired philosophy, expressed visually
This is DHS's adaptation, not a claim about Hormozi's endorsement or an exact quotation.
- HOOK: create a visible reason to watch the next action. A headline, logo or random camera move is not enough by itself.
- RETAIN: establish an expectation and progress towards it through meaningful actions, changes, humour, sound and pauses.
- REWARD: fulfil the opening's promise with a reveal, satisfying result, useful distinction or genuine human moment.
- PROOF: show what the clinic actually does; distinguish real evidence from illustration.
- LOW FRICTION: make the next action clear and proportionate. One CTA, no forced engagement gate or fake scarcity.

The reward need not be a mini-lecture. Keep clinical qualifications wherever required for truth and safety. Never withhold urgent advice to preserve suspense.

## 3. New production order
**Topic and job → visual concepts → opening prototype → storyboard/sound plan → capture/generation → picture-led edit → minimal copy → QA/approval → learning.**
Do not start with a long script and search for background footage afterwards.

### Gate A — lock the brief
Record the planned date, topic, format, objective, intended audience, single viewer payoff and one relevant clinic connection. Preserve the approved calendar's CTA unless the owner approves a change.
Identify the intended emotion: curiosity, delight, anticipation, recognition, reassurance or satisfaction. Not fear, disgust or humiliation.
Inspect available assets before promising a shot. Separate existing material, new capture, stock/licensed assets, generated imagery and exact graphics.

### Gate B — brainstorm three genuinely different visual mechanisms
Use three of these, not three headlines over the same clip:
| Mechanism | Opening question in the viewer's mind | Production option |
|---|---|---|
| Prediction / choice | Which one? What happens next? | Two props, a visible test, immediate action |
| Concealed reveal | What is under/behind/inside that? | Mirror, lift, peel, slide, turn or motivated mask |
| Transformation | What will this become? | Real process, stop motion or clearly illustrative animation |
| Satisfying craft | Will that fit/align/finish? | Macro hands, texture, precision, gradual completion |
| Scale / perspective surprise | Is that really that size? | Forced perspective or an openly illustrative constructed scene |
| Everyday comedy | I recognise this situation—what goes wrong? | Consenting staff/prop performance, never patient ridicule |

For each candidate write: FIRST FRAME → EXPECTED ACTION → CHANGE/TWIST → PAYOFF → DHS CONNECTION.
A visual hook may use a short label to orient the viewer. The test is whether the action is interesting without reading a paragraph, not whether absolutely all text is absent.
Reference million-view examples for craft, not causal proof; note celebrity reach, account baseline and paid status when known. Never copy another creator's footage, signature performance or wording wholesale.

### Gate C — build the opening before the full asset
Make a 2–3-second rough prototype or representative frame sequence. Test:
- Is the focal object recognisable at phone size?
- Is an action already starting, or a meaningful event visibly imminent?
- Can someone say what they expect to happen next?
- Does the reveal follow naturally from the setup?
- Will the promise remain honest when connected to the clinic?
- Does the idea remain interesting on mute?
Reject a weak premise before spending on polished graphics. A prototype is not approval to publish or a guarantee of performance.

### Gate D — storyboard picture and sound together
Before full animation/editing, lock a compact motion language for the piece:
- tone: calm ↔ kinetic; soft ↔ sharp,
- timing scale: fast / medium / slow behaviours,
- one main easing behaviour,
- one main transition family,
- stagger rhythm if type/objects sequence,
- motion hierarchy: **Hero / Support / Texture**,
- what will intentionally remain still.

These are project-specific decisions, not universal numeric presets. If motion has no job, remove it.

Use this shot sheet for every Reel:
| Time | Subject/action | Camera/framing | Expectation/change/payoff | Source: real/AI/graphic | Sound/voice/pause | Minimal text | Continuity/privacy |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

A flexible starting rhythm, not a template:
- 0–2s: visual event / prediction.
- 2–5s: progress, escalation or reveal.
- 5–10s: payoff and honest bridge to the service/topic.
- Closing: brief relevant next action.
Let the action determine runtime. Start by exploring roughly 8–18 seconds for a simple visual concept; longer is justified when the payoff genuinely requires it. No compulsory cut every second, countdown, reveal or voiceover.
Keep visual progress after the hook. Do not replace an engaging scene with a long stack of text cards.
For carousels: open on a visual question/contrast, let subsequent slides resolve it, and use concise decision support; no padded slide count.
For Stories: one visible event or genuine moment per frame, then a relevant low-effort interaction or booking step. Not every Story needs cinema or a sales pitch.

### Gate E — capture or create
Owner receives ONE compact asset request, listing essential versus optional:
- item; precise framing/action; clip length; same-case/continuity requirement; consent; whether a still is sufficient.
Batch captures in one short session when practical. Hands/props/clinic details can lead. Do not require the owner to perform on camera.
Capture handles before/after each action, stable light and focus, close/medium/context coverage, plus clean natural sound where appropriate.
Do not stage clinical care, compromise infection control, repeat a procedure or delay treatment for filming. Demonstrations use separate props/models.

### Gate F — edit the visual sequence first
1. Assemble the action without headline cards. Remove dead setup.
2. Ensure each shot adds a change, expectation, detail, reaction or payoff.
3. Design sound: real contact/texture, selected Foley, music, voice and intentional silence. Sync sound to the actual event, not every edit. No fake patient distress or invented treatment sounds presented as real.
4. Add concise labels, necessary subtitles and qualifications. Text does not narrate every visible movement.
5. Integrate DHS identity, caption and one CTA. Avoid a long detached closing card.
6. Export and inspect the actual file at phone size, with sound and muted. If audio cannot be heard in the review environment, mark audio QA pending; do not claim it passed.

### Gate G — deliver and verify the actual output
Do not approve from the editing timeline alone.

For **carousels/static sequences**:
- inspect the cover alone for a 2-second first impression,
- inspect every slide at phone size,
- inspect all slide thumbnails together for progression, repetition and continuity,
- run without-logo, asset-role, proof-truth and CTA tests.

For **Reels/video**:
- inspect the encoded/exported video, not only a preview,
- make a representative contact sheet from the actual export when tooling allows; use frames around the opening (roughly 0s / 1s / 2s), midpoint, payoff and final CTA, adapting the sampling to the actual runtime,
- compare those frames for repeated composition, dead visual states, motion hierarchy, text density and whether the payoff is visually earned,
- watch once sound-on and once muted,
- confirm kinetic type supports meaning/rhythm rather than substituting for the footage,
- check that transitions are motivated and the piece does not resemble a transition pack.

Then run the cold-review tags from `DHS-CREATIVE-LOOP.md`. Fix the highest-impact diagnosed layer before adding polish.

**Output-over-compliance rule:** following the storyboard, motion plan or design checklist does not make an output good. If the assembled piece still feels generic, dated, AI-made or unclear, it is not finished.


## 4. AI, motion graphics and real proof
| Material | Allowed role | Boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Real footage/photos | Clinic, staff, examination, actual findings, outcomes, community | Consent scope, de-identification, no alteration of findings |
| AI / constructed scene | Fictional props/worlds, imaginative opening, scale illusion, transitions | Clearly illustrative; never fake patients, clinic capability, testimonials or treatment outcomes |
| Exact graphics / 3D | Models, assembly, diagrammatic transitions, visual comparisons | Anatomy/measurements/labels checked; use controlled assets when accuracy matters |
| Motion graphics | Masking, object tracking, meaningful transformation, spatial typography, sequencing | One coherent visual language; not random spins, glitch packs or motion on every element |
| Sound / voice | Anticipation, contact, reveal, warmth, clarity | Rights verified, intelligible mix; generated voice not presented as a real patient |

Choose real, generated or hybrid based on the concept, not an arbitrary percentage.
Never match-cut unrelated AI and patient imagery so it falsely appears to be the same case or a before/after. Small disclaimers do not rescue a misleading sequence; redesign it if necessary.
No newly generated AI assets are part of this workflow-writing task. Check available tools, costs and consent at production time.

## 5. Design retained; expression expanded

Use the latest owner-confirmed master identity and inspect the actual supplied logo before production; do not select identity from old defaults.

Restraint means deliberate choices, not static footage. Premium can be warm, lively and surprising. Cinematic means staging, light, composition, action and sound—not darkness, slow motion or letterboxing by default.

Clinical colours/findings are not beautified or worsened for impact. Avoid shame labels such as dirty patients.
